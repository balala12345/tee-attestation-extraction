# Redmi K70E: Full practice ledger (sanitized)

> **Documentation provenance and reproduction note:** The organization, splitting, translation, sanitization, and chapter arrangement of this directory were produced by an AI reading the existing project tree, source documents, practice ledgers, and artifact manifests. The public copy does not represent manual execution, independent reproduction, or sign-off for every command, patch, image, or result. “Tutorial,” “result,” and “acceptance” refer to source records, evidence models, or AI-organized conclusions; they do not mean that the public copy has been independently validated, and they are not guaranteed to work outside the complete project context. The intended workflow is to have an AI read the complete target-device directory and produce prerequisites, version/hash gates, invalid routes, missing evidence, and steps requiring validation, then have an authorized human review each item in an isolated environment. Never execute an AI-generated command without human review.


1. The initial native CSR and Google issuance baseline established the request structure, DICE chain layout, DeviceInfo fields, and certificate-chain validation rules.
2. Repeated diagnostics distinguished UDS, CDI leaf, BCC seed, active RKP private-key objects, opaque key blobs, and arbitrary 32-byte buffers. The key rule became: a candidate is accepted only when its derived P-256 public key matches the final leaf in the same CSR.
3. Static closure of the key-generation and cleanup paths was completed. Multiple hook and patched-TEE variants failed because of ABI, timing, compression-frame, or boot-chain contracts. Logo loops and recovery events were retained as failure evidence.
4. A patched route later exported native RKP scalars and obtained Google responses, but its signed DeviceInfo/DICE context produced `NOT_APPROVED`. The result was rejected even though the private key and certificate chain were cryptographically valid.
5. Differential analysis showed that the approved and rejected requests differed in signed DeviceInfo as well as in portions of the DICE chain. The approval gate was changed to preserve unknown Provisioning Info keys, reject non-canonical/duplicate CBOR, and compare the signed fields rather than relying on HLOS properties.
6. A clean-DeviceInfo host-only controlled run reused the exact modified DICE source and the matching identity scalar, generated one fresh software key, and obtained HTTP 200 from both Google endpoints. The returned four-certificate chain had `APPROVED` and zero `NOT_APPROVED` hits.
7. The run was classified `PASS_CLEAN_HYBRID`: cryptographically closed and approved, but not a fully-stock same-run device claim. The device was restored and the submission verifier, provenance, and SHA-256 manifests were frozen.
8. The delivered copy keeps every selected source record, script, image, firmware package, failure log, and validation artifact. It removes serials, host/user paths, raw scalars, private keys, and raw identity/response bodies.
